# Process: ordered human and automated steps

Processing is GetWorkDone's second verb (GWP#5, spec §4.3). A work type's definition lists its steps in execution order; a captured item moves through them, one transition at a time, to `completed`, which distribution (GWP#6) takes as its trigger. Every transform is deterministic and there is no LLM (D-028). Decisions are cited by number from the program register (spec §9.4) and never copied here. The entities are in [domain.md](domain.md) and capture in [capture.md](capture.md).

## Step kinds

`ProcessStep` (`packages/contracts/src/domain.ts`) is one of:

- **human**: `{ id, name, kind: 'human', assigneeRole, decisions? }`. The item waits at it until a team member holding `assigneeRole` completes it. An admin holds `member` too, so an admin may complete any human step; a member may complete only `member` steps. `decisions` (GWP#54) optionally lists what the person completing it may decide, such as `approve` and `reject` (see Decisions).
- **automated**: `{ id, name, kind: 'automated', transform, params }`. It runs as soon as the item reaches it: `transform` names an entry in the transform registry and `params` are that transform's parameters.

A work type with no steps is allowed: its items are completed as soon as they are captured.

## How an item moves

Capture creates the item (`open`, event `captured`) and hands it to `ProcessService.advance` (`apps/api/src/modules/process/process.service.ts`), which runs consecutive automated steps until it reaches a human step or the end:

1. **Start** the current step: status `in_progress`, event `step_started`.
2. An automated step then **runs**: on success its output becomes the item's data and the item moves to the next step (`step_completed`); on failure the item is `failed` and stays at that step (`step_failed`, with the reason).
3. A human step **waits**. `POST .../steps/:stepId/complete` writes its `step_completed`, with the decision and comment when given, and the item advances again.
4. After the last step the item is **completed** (`completed`). The `completed` transition applies the item's outputs (GWP#6, [outputs.md](outputs.md)): it writes one `Delivery` row per email or webhook output and, for output `none`, clears the data. Then `ProcessService.onItemCompleted(item)` fires once and wakes the delivery worker; an error it throws is logged and leaves the item completed, and the worker's poll still finds the rows.

The engine decides each move from the item's status, its `currentStepIndex` and the type of its last transition event (`apps/api/src/modules/process/engine.ts`), which every transition writes together. An admin's data edit (`data_edited`, see Editing data) moves no step, so the engine and the item view read past it. `currentStepIndex` is the step the item is at; after the last step it equals the number of steps.

Every transition also keeps `Item.waitingRole` (GWP#55): the transition that starts a human step sets it to the step's `assigneeRole`, and every other transition clears it, except a data edit, which moves no step and leaves it as it was. So `waitingRole` is non-null exactly while the item is `in_progress` waiting at a started human step, and the waiting-for-me queue (see The API) and the stuck-item sweeper (see Resume) read it with a `WHERE`. The migration that added it (`20261031120000_waiting_role_and_archive`) set it for items already waiting: `in_progress`, the current step of their version's definition a human step, and their last event, a `data_edited` aside, its `step_started`. An item `advance` finds waiting at a started human step without the step's role in `waitingRole` (a step started by a task still running the older code during a deploy) gets the role in place, with no event and no `rowVersion` change, and a warning log line; so it joins the queue, and the sweeper, which lists it once, counts it `skipped` and does not list it again.

Instead of any of these, an admin may **cancel** an item that has not completed (`cancelled`, see Cancel), which ends it where it stands: no output is applied and the data is kept.

### Transitions, idempotency and concurrency

Each transition is one transaction, at `READ COMMITTED`: an `UPDATE "Item" ... WHERE id = $id AND "teamId" = $team AND "rowVersion" = $expected` that also increments `rowVersion`, then one `ItemEvent` with the next `seq`. The update comes first, so it holds the item's row lock while the next `seq` is read; an item's events therefore have no gaps in `seq`. A request whose `rowVersion` is stale, or that loses a race to another, matches no row, writes nothing and is answered 409. Twenty parallel completions of one step give one 200 and nineteen 409, and one `step_completed` row (`apps/api/test/process.integration.test.ts`).

Each transition runs inside one span, `process.step`, with the attributes `process.transition` (the event type), `step.kind`, `step.id` and, for automated steps, `transform.name`. The `completed` transition's span carries `process.transition` only. A refused request (403, 404, 409) does not mark a span as an error.

### Events

| Event | Payload | `actorSubject` |
|---|---|---|
| `step_started` | `stepId`, `stepIndex`, `kind`, and `transform` (automated) or `assigneeRole` (human); `retry: true` on a retry | the person whose request reached the step for a human step (the admin, on a resume; null when the stuck-item sweeper started it, GWP#55); the admin on a retry; null for automated steps |
| `step_completed` | `stepId`, `stepIndex`, `kind`, `transform` (automated); for a human step, `decision` and `comment` when the person gave them (GWP#54) | the person who completed a human step; null for automated steps |
| `step_failed` | `stepId`, `stepIndex`, `kind`, `transform`, `reason`, and `errors` (field key -> messages) when the output failed validation | null |
| `completed` | `{}`, or `{ dataCleared: true }` for output `none` | the person whose request completed the item (the admin, on a resume; null when the stuck-item sweeper completed it, GWP#55) |
| `data_edited` (GWP#23) | `fields`: the keys whose values were added, removed or changed, sorted | the admin who edited the data |
| `cancelled` (GWP#54) | `{}`, or `{ reason }` when the admin gave one | the admin who cancelled the item |
| `delivery_failed` (GWP#6) | `outputId`, `kind`, `attempts`, `reason` | null |

Payloads name steps and fields, never captured values, so the audit trail keeps no data that output `none` clears (D-048). A decision, a comment and a cancel's reason are what a person wrote about the item, not captured values: they live on the event only, are never written to the data, and survive clearing like every event.

### Retry

A failed item can be retried by a team admin: `POST .../steps/:stepId/retry` with the item's `rowVersion`, for the step it failed at. Each call re-runs that step once (`step_started` with `retry: true`, then `step_completed` or `step_failed`); on success the item advances as usual. A retry of a step the item did not fail at, or of an item that has not failed, is 409.

A retry sees the item's data as it stands, so a failure that depends on the data (a `require_fields` failure, or a transform whose output failed validation) needs the data corrected first: see Editing data.

### Editing data

A team admin corrects an item's data with `POST .../items/:itemId/data` and `{ rowVersion, data }` (GWP#23), while the item is `failed`, or `in_progress` waiting at a started human step. `data` is the item's whole new data, checked with `validateCapture` against the fields of the item's own version (not the work type's latest), as at capture. The edit is one transition like the others: the data replaced and one `data_edited` event naming the changed keys, never their values (D-048), inside a `process.step` span with `process.transition` `data_edited` and the current step's attributes. The item's status and step do not change: a failed item is then retried, and a waiting step still waits for its completion, which sees the new data.

Every refusal writes nothing: a stale `rowVersion` is 409, any other state (`open`, `completed`, or `in_progress` and not waiting at a human step) is 409, and then data that fails validation is 422 `{ errors }` keyed by field. Data that changes nothing writes nothing and answers the item as it is. Members cannot edit data, and a human step's completion carries no data (GWP#23 "Out of scope").

### Decisions

A human step may list `decisions`: 1 to 10 texts of 1 to 40 characters, no two alike (GWP#54). The person completing the step sends one of them as `decision`, with an optional `comment` of up to 2000 characters, in `POST .../steps/:stepId/complete`. A decision not in the list, or none, is 422 `{ "errors": { "decision": ["Choose one of: approve, reject"] } }` and writes nothing; it is checked after the role (403) and the 409s. A step without `decisions` accepts any `decision` text of 1 to 40 characters, or none, and `{ rowVersion }` alone completes it as before.

Both are written on the step's `step_completed` event and nowhere else: nothing goes in the item's data, so neither the fields' validation nor output `none`'s clearing (D-048) applies. A decision is a recorded value, never a branch: the item moves to the next step whatever it is (D-028).

### Cancel

A team admin cancels an item with `POST .../items/:itemId/cancel` and `{ rowVersion, reason? }` (GWP#54; `reason` up to 2000 characters), while it is `open`, `in_progress` (waiting at a human step or not) or `failed`. The cancel is one transition: status `cancelled` and one `cancelled` event with the reason when given and the admin as actor, inside a `process.step` span with `process.transition` `cancelled` and the current step's attributes. Every refusal writes nothing: a stale `rowVersion` is 409, then a `completed` or `cancelled` item is 409 `This item is already completed` (or `cancelled`).

No output fires and the data is kept: output `none` clears data at completion only (D-048), and a cancelled item never completes. Once cancelled, nothing moves the item again: `complete`, `retry`, `advance` and a data edit each answer 409 and write nothing, with `This item is already cancelled` for a caller holding the item's current `rowVersion` (one holding an older one gets the stale-read message, as everywhere). A cancelled item cannot be reopened (GWP#54 "Out of scope").

### Resume

Each transition commits on its own, so a request that ends between two of them (the process stops, the connection pool times out, the database drops) leaves the item where the last one left it, and nothing moves it on by itself: `open` with only its `captured` event, or `in_progress` with `step_completed`, or an automated step's `step_started`, as its last event. The engine reads those states as "start the current step", "run it" or "complete the item", the same as the request that stopped would have (GWP#22).

A team admin resumes such an item with `POST .../items/:itemId/advance` and the item's `rowVersion`. It calls `advance` from where the item stands, so the item ends where one uninterrupted request would have left it, with no gap in its events' `seq`; the admin is the actor of a human step's `step_started` and of `completed`. Every refusal is 409 and comes before any write: a stale `rowVersion`, an item waiting at a human step it has started (`This item is waiting at step "Review" for a member to complete it`), a `failed` item (retry its step instead) and a `completed` one.

Capture is the request most likely to stop this way, as it processes the item it has just created. When processing throws once the item exists, capture logs the error, records it on its `capture.submit` span (attribute `capture.processing_failed`) and still answers 201 with the item as it stands, so the submitter does not capture it twice ([capture.md](capture.md)); only when the item cannot even be read back is the answer a 500. A 409 there means an admin's resume won the race and is logged as a warning.

The stuck-item sweeper (`apps/api/src/modules/process/stuck-item-sweeper.ts`, GWP#55) resumes such items without an admin. It runs in every API task, once at startup and then every `STUCK_ITEM_SWEEP_MS` (300000, 5 minutes): it lists up to 50 items that are `open` or `in_progress` with `waitingRole` null (not waiting at a human step) and not updated for `STUCK_ITEM_AFTER_MS` (600000, 10 minutes, longer than any transaction or delivery deadline, so no live request is still moving them), oldest update first (an item it has backed off comes after the rest, see below), and calls `advance` on each with the `rowVersion` it listed and no actor. A human step it starts and the `completed` it writes then have `actorSubject: null`. An item that is `failed` waits for an admin's retry and is never listed. The list is read through the partial index `Item_status_updatedAt_waiting_null_idx` (`("status", "updatedAt") WHERE "waitingRole" IS NULL`, GWP#157), which holds no item waiting at a human step, so the sweep no longer walks every old item parked at one; the unfiltered `Item_status_updatedAt_idx` stayed for the previous image until `20261103180000_drop_item_status_updated_at_idx` (GWP#158) dropped it (expand, then contract). The statuses reach Postgres cast from text, which an index predicate on `status` could not match, so `status` is the index's first key, not its condition. On a fresh instance, or one whose new image started before its migration, it first waits for its schema (GWP#101, GWP#127): the column `Item.sweepRetryAt`, the newest of those the sweep reads and writes. `20261102000000_sweep_backoff` adds it in one statement with `sweepFailures`, after the migration that adds `waitingRole` (and added the `Item_status_updatedAt_idx` index, since dropped by GWP#158), so once it exists the sweep's filter and backoff have every column they use. It does not wait for the partial index: without it the sweep is slower, not wrong. (Until GWP#127 it waited for that index, which a database migrated up to the index but not the backoff already had, and every sweep then failed on the missing column until the migration ran.) The check reads `pg_catalog.pg_attribute`, which needs no privilege on the table (`information_schema.columns` lists only the columns of tables the role holds a privilege on). It checks at once and then every minute, logs `stuck item sweeper waiting for the database schema` once at `warn` (with `table: "Item.sweepRetryAt"`) and `stuck item sweeper started` when the column exists. Each variable takes a positive whole number of milliseconds, or its default; under `NODE_ENV=test` the sweeper does not run on its own and tests call `StuckItemSweeper.runOnce`.

Several tasks may sweep at once: each advance matches on the listed `rowVersion`, so an item moves once and the other sweep's 409 is counted `skipped`, as is an item someone else moved first or one the advance had nothing to do for. Any other error is logged (`stuck item sweep could not advance an item`, with the item id), counted `failed`, and the sweep goes on. Each run is a `process.sweep` span, the counters `process_sweeper_runs_total{status}` and `process_sweeper_items_total{result="resumed"|"skipped"|"failed"}` (`recordProcessSweep` in `@getworkdone/telemetry`), and one log line, `stuck item sweep`, with `items_resumed`, `items_skipped`, `items_failed` and `duration_ms`; like every record they carry the four resource attributes (D-044). A run whose listing fails is logged (`stuck item sweep failed`), counted `status="error"` and tried again on the next interval.

An item whose advance failed is backed off (GWP#102), so items that fail every time (a definition the engine cannot run, a row the database refuses) never fill the batch ahead of a real stopped item. The sweeper sets `Item.sweepRetryAt` to the sweep's time plus `STUCK_ITEM_AFTER_MS` doubled for each failure in a row after the first (10, 20, 40, 80 minutes, and so on), at most 24 hours from the ninth failure on, and adds one to `Item.sweepFailures`; it writes no event and changes neither `rowVersion` nor `updatedAt`, as the item has not moved. It lists only items whose `sweepRetryAt` is null or past, so it tries such an item again no sooner than that, and lists those it has backed off after every item it has not tried yet (fewest `sweepFailures` first, then oldest update), so items that fail again never take the batch from one that has not had its turn. (While 50 or more untried items are waiting at every sweep, a backed-off item is not tried again until they are gone; by its fifth failure its error line has handed it to a person anyway.) The write matches the `rowVersion` and `sweepFailures` it listed, so a transition since, or another task's backoff of the same item, wins. Any transition (a step started or completed, a retry, a resume, a data edit, a cancel) sets `sweepRetryAt` back to null and `sweepFailures` to 0. If the backoff write itself fails, that is logged (`stuck item sweep could not back off an item`) and the item is listed again on the next run.

The failed advance's log line carries `sweep_failures` (this failure's count) and `sweep_retry_at`. It is a warning for the first four failures in a row; from the fifth on it is an `error`, which is when the sweeper hands the item to a person: an admin reads the line's `err` and the item, corrects what stops it (or cancels it), and resumes it, or waits for the next try. The sweeper never moves an item to `failed` on its own. `process_sweeper_items_total{result="failed"}` counts every failed advance as before.

So an `open` item whose processing stopped is moved on within `STUCK_ITEM_AFTER_MS + STUCK_ITEM_SWEEP_MS` of its last update, with nobody pressing anything, while fewer than 50 items the sweeper has not tried yet are ahead of it. Items that keep failing do not hold it back: once tried, they wait out their backoff and then queue behind every untried item. Only many items stopping or breaking at once delays it, by one sweep for each further 50 untried items older than it (100 items that always fail and a stopped item behind them: the item moves on the third sweep after its cut-off; `apps/api/test/stuck-item-sweeper.integration.test.ts`). Resume stays for a faster hand: an admin who sees a stopped item need not wait for the sweep.

## Transform catalogue

The registry is `TRANSFORMS` in `apps/api/src/modules/process/transforms.ts`. Each transform's `params` schema is the contract's (`TRANSFORM_PARAMS` in `packages/contracts/src/process.ts`), so the API and the builder share it. A transform is pure: it returns the new data and never changes its input or does I/O. Its output is re-validated with `validateCapture` against the item's version's fields ([capture.md](capture.md)); output that fails is a failed step with the validation's errors.

| Transform | Params | What it does | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `set_field` | `field`, `value` (a string, number or boolean) | Sets `field` to the constant `value`. | The output fails validation. |
| `copy_field` | `from`, `to` | Copies `from`'s value to `to`; if `from` is absent, `to` is removed. | The output fails validation (for example a number copied into a text field). |
| `string_case` | `field` (text or longtext), `op`: `upper`, `lower` or `trim` | Changes the case of `field`, or trims it. An absent field stays absent. | The value is not text: `Field "x" is not text`. |
| `arithmetic` | `op`: `add`, `subtract` or `multiply`; `left`, `right`, `into` (number fields) | Sets `into` to `left op right`. | An operand is empty (`Field "x" is empty`) or not a number, or the result is not finite. |
| `require_fields` | `fields` (one or more) | Passes the data through unchanged. | Any listed field is empty, meaning absent, null or blank text: `Required fields are empty: a, b`. |

The step's stored params are parsed again before it runs (stored definitions are not re-parsed when read); params that no longer parse, or a transform no longer in the registry, fail the step rather than the request.

### Checks at save

Every write of a definition (`POST .../work-types` and `POST .../work-types/:id/versions`) checks its steps with the contract's `stepProblems` against the registry, after the definition itself parses:

- every step id, human or automated, must be 1 to 64 letters, digits, dashes and underscores, because it is a URL path segment (`.../steps/:stepId/complete`); the step editor generates `step_N`;
- the transform must be in the registry (`Unknown transform "x"`);
- the params must parse with its schema;
- every field a param names must be in the definition, of the types the param takes (`No field "x" in this work type`, `Must be a field of type number`);
- a `set_field` value must be a valid value for its field (the field's own capture messages).

A failure is 422 `{ "errors": { "definition.steps.0.transform": [...] } }`, keyed by path, on both routes, and writes nothing. (A definition the contract itself refuses is 422 `{ errors }` on both routes too, as in [capture.md](capture.md).)

## The API

Every route is under the team checks in [auth.md](auth.md): 401 without a session, 403 for a non-member, and the `X-GWD-CSRF` header on writes.

| Route | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId` | member | The item, its events, and `steps` and `fields`: the steps and fields of the version it was captured under. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId/steps/:stepId/complete` | a member holding the step's `assigneeRole` | `{ rowVersion, decision?, comment? }` (`CompleteStepRequest`; GWP#54): completes the human step the item is waiting at, then advances. 200 with the item as above. 403 for the wrong role, 404 for an unknown item or step, 409 for a stale `rowVersion`, a cancelled item or a step that is not waiting, 422 keyed `decision` for a decision the step's `decisions` lack (see Decisions), 400 without a `rowVersion`, with an unknown key, or for a `decision` over 40 or a `comment` over 2000 characters. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId/steps/:stepId/retry` | admin | `{ rowVersion }`: re-runs the failed step once, then advances. 200 with the item; 409 when the item has not failed at that step, is cancelled, or the `rowVersion` is stale. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId/cancel` | admin | `{ rowVersion, reason? }` (`CancelItemRequest`; GWP#54): cancels an `open`, `in_progress` or `failed` item (see Cancel). 200 with the item, `status: 'cancelled'`; 409 for a stale `rowVersion` or a `completed` or `cancelled` item, with a message naming the status; 404 for an unknown item; 400 without a `rowVersion`, with an unknown key or a `reason` over 2000 characters. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId/data` | admin | `{ rowVersion, data }`: replaces the data of a `failed` item, or of one waiting at a human step (see Editing data). 200 with the item; 422 `{ errors }` for data its version's fields refuse; 409 for a stale `rowVersion` or any other state; 404 for an unknown item; 400 for a body without both. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId/advance` | admin | `{ rowVersion }`: resumes an item whose processing stopped between transitions (see Resume). 200 with the item; 409 for a stale `rowVersion` or an item that is waiting at a human step, `failed`, `completed` or `cancelled`, with a message saying which; 404 for an unknown item; 400 without a `rowVersion`. |
| `GET /api/v1/teams/:teamId/items?waiting=me&cursor=&status=` | member | The waiting-for-me queue (GWP#55): the team's `in_progress` items waiting at a human step whose `assigneeRole` the caller holds in the team (a member holds `member`; an admin holds `admin` and `member`), across every work type, newest first, as an `ItemPage` of at most 100 with `nextCursor` (the same cursor as a work type's list). `?status=` narrows it as on a work type's list; only `in_progress` items ever wait. 400 without `?waiting=me` (there is no team-wide list of every item), for a repeated parameter, a bad cursor or an unknown status. A `WHERE` on `Item.waitingRole` beside the cursor, through the index `Item(teamId, waitingRole, createdAt, id)`. |
| `POST /api/v1/teams/:teamId/work-types/:id/archive` and `.../unarchive` | admin | `{}` (`ArchiveWorkTypeRequest`; GWP#55): archives or unarchives the work type; 200 with the work type, `archivedAt` set or null. Archiving an archived one keeps its first `archivedAt`; unarchiving an active one changes nothing and is 200 at any count. Archived work types do not count against `QUOTA_WORK_TYPES_PER_TEAM` (GWP#103), so unarchiving an archived one while the team has its cap of active work types is 409 `This team has reached its limit of 50 work types` and leaves it archived ([domain.md](domain.md) "Limits and quotas"). While archived, capture (`POST .../items`) and a new version (`POST .../versions`) are 409 `This work type is archived`, and `GET .../work-types` leaves it out unless `?includeArchived=true`. Its items, the item view, its item list, exports and downloads stay readable, and items in flight go on to completion and delivery (neither the engine nor the delivery worker looks at `archivedAt`). 404 for an unknown work type or another team's, 400 for a body with any key. See [capture.md](capture.md). |

The work type's item list, `GET .../work-types/:id/items`, takes `?status=` (one `ItemStatus`) beside `?cursor=` to list only the items in that status (GWP#54, [capture.md](capture.md)).

Capture's `POST .../work-types/:id/items` answers the item after processing, with its `steps`: `in_progress` at a human step, `failed`, or `completed`; or, when processing failed after the item was created, the item where it stopped (see Resume).

## In the SPA

- **Step editor** (`apps/web/src/work-types/builder/steps/`, in the builder, admins): add human and automated steps, set a human step's assignee role and its Decisions (one per line, 1 to 10, each 1 to 40 characters; blank lines are dropped and a duplicate is refused before the save; GWP#54), choose an automated step's transform, reorder with Move up and Move down (keyboard accessible, announced) and remove. The parameter form is generated from the transform's Zod params (`describeParams`): a field param is a list of the definition's fields of the types it takes, an enum is a list of its options, a list of fields is a set of checkboxes, and a constant takes its field's type. The builder runs `stepProblems` as it goes and shows each message beside its input.
- **Waiting for you** (`apps/web/src/work-types/WaitingForYou.tsx`, GWP#55): at the top of the team page's list view, the items waiting for the signed-in member, with their count (the items loaded so far, followed by `+` while more pages remain) and a Load more. Each row shows when the item was captured and its first captured values; Open shows the item view, and Back returns to the team page, which reloads the list.
- **Archive** (`apps/web/src/work-types/TeamWorkTypes.tsx`, GWP#55): admins get Archive beside each work type in the team page's list. Archived work types move to a collapsed Archived work types section, where Items opens the capture page without its form (its items and exports stay readable) and, for admins, Unarchive brings it back. A capture page already open on a work type that is then archived shows the 409 as the form's error.
- **Item view** (`apps/web/src/items/item-view.tsx`, opened with Open in the capture page's item list or the Waiting for you list, at its own address `/teams/:teamId/items/:itemId`, whose Back to the items returns to that list, or to the team page for a pasted link; GWP#98, [web.md](web.md) "Addresses"): the status and data, the steps with their state (done, running, waiting for a member or an admin, failed, pending), and the event history. It offers Complete on a waiting step to those who hold its role, Retry on a failed step to admins, Resume to admins on an item whose processing stopped (`open`, or `in_progress` and not waiting at a started human step; GWP#22), and Edit data to admins on a failed item or one waiting at a human step: the capture form, prefilled with the item's data, whose Save data sends the edit (GWP#23). While the form is open the view's other actions wait; a stale view closes it, shows why and reloads the item. History shows a `data_edited` as the names of the fields it changed. Beside each Complete button (GWP#54) is a Decision, a select of the step's decisions when it lists them or else free text, and a Comment box; the history shows a `step_completed`'s decision and comment. Admins get Cancel item beside Resume while the item is `open`, `in_progress` or `failed`: it opens a Reason box (optional) and Confirm cancel or Keep the item, and the history shows the reason. A cancelled item's current step reads cancelled and the view offers no action on it. A human step the item reached but never started reads pending until it is resumed. A stale view is refused, its message shown, and the item reloaded.

## Not here yet

- Scheduled or time-based steps, and user-supplied code transforms: out of scope.
- Assigning an item to a person, and notifications when an item waits (GWP#55 "Out of scope").
